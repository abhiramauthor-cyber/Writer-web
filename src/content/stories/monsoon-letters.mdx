The rains came early that year, drumming against the corrugated tin roof of the veranda. Inside, she traced the edge of the envelope. It was sealed, but she knew the shape of his handwriting even through the paper. 

She had found a stack of them in the back of his closet, hidden behind winter coats that hadn't been worn since they moved to Chennai. Six letters. None of them addressed, none of them stamped. But they all started the same way: *To the woman who stayed.*

She didn't open them. Instead, she took the first one and slipped it into the spine of the poetry collection she kept on her bedside table. Over the next month, she distributed the rest. One in a cookbook. One in a dictionary. One in a novel about a war that neither of them had lived through.

She liked knowing they were there. It felt like finding a second pulse in the house, a quiet thrumming underneath the floorboards. She didn't need to read them to know what they said. The weight of them was enough.
