The stainless steel tumbler was hot to the touch. He poured the dark liquid back and forth between the tumbler and the davara, watching the foam rise and settle. One meter, exactly. That was the trick.

He placed the first cup on the table, next to the folded newspaper. The second cup he placed opposite it, in the space where the morning light always hit first.

He sat down and drank his coffee in silence. When he finished, he picked up the newspaper and began to read. The second cup sat there, cooling, until the foam dissolved and the dark liquid settled into a still, quiet pool. 

At eight o'clock, he would take both cups to the sink and wash them. He never drank the second cup. He never poured it back into the pot. He just washed it away, down the drain, and put the tumbler back on the shelf, ready for tomorrow.
